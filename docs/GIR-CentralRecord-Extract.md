## 1. UAE's status in the Central Record — confirmed qualified

**Entry:** United Arab Emirates · *Cabinet Decision No. 142 of 2024 on the Imposition of Top-up Tax on Multinational Enterprises* · **Yes** · effective **1 January 2025**

This appears in the **"Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax Rules and QDMTT Safe Harbours"** table — confirming CD 142 has:
- **Qualified DMTT status**, and
- **QDMTT Safe Harbour** availability

Effective from 1 January 2025, matching CD 142's own application date.

**Why this matters practically:** because the UAE's DMTT is *qualified*, an MNE Group's Top-up Tax computed elsewhere (under an IIR in the UPE's jurisdiction) can be reduced to zero for the UAE via the QDMTT Safe Harbour. This is a selling point for UAE entities and a genuine reassurance worth surfacing in a tool — the UAE regime is recognised, not provisional.

**Also confirmed:** the UAE is **not** listed as a Conditional DMTT or Elective DMTT for 2024 (those categories concern 2024 starters; the UAE began 2025). The Central Record notes an MNE Group cannot claim the QDMTT Safe Harbour if it is not subject to an Elective DMTT — not applicable to the UAE.

**Maintenance note:** the Central Record is explicitly a **rolling document**, updated as jurisdictions complete the transitional qualification mechanism. The version in the folder is current as at 1 May 2026. Worth periodic re-checking, same as TTGSHO1.

## 2. GIR dissemination model — the UAE is a "QDMTT-only Jurisdiction"

The GIR explicitly recognises two categories: **Implementing Jurisdictions** (IIR and/or UTPR) and **QDMTT-only Jurisdictions**. The UAE is the latter — it receives a **narrower slice** of the GIR than a full Implementing Jurisdiction.

**What each category receives (GIR Introduction ¶19–23):**
| Recipient | Receives |
|---|---|
| UPE's jurisdiction | **The whole GIR** |
| Jurisdictions with taxing rights (incl. QDMTT jurisdictions) | Sections relating to ETR and Top-up Tax computation, allocation and attribution for the jurisdictions over which they have taxing rights |
| All Implementing Jurisdictions where CEs are located | General information + corporate structure |
| **QDMTT-only Jurisdiction (the UAE)** | **Section 1 (General Section) — but EXCLUDING Section 1.4** (the high-level GloBE summary), subject to conditions below |

**Conditions for a QDMTT-only Jurisdiction to receive Section 1** (any one of):
(i) Constituent Entities of the MNE Group are located in that jurisdiction; or
(ii) a JV or member of a JV Group is located there **and** the QDMTT is imposed on JVs located there; or
(iii) the QDMTT is imposed on a Stateless Constituent Entity or Stateless JV of the MNE Group

**Specifically withheld from QDMTT-only Jurisdictions:** Section 1.4's high-level summary — which shows, per jurisdiction, whether Top-up Tax is payable and in which band (no tax · <€1m · <€5m · <€25m · <€50m · <€75m · <€100m · <€250m · ≥€250m). The GIR states plainly: *"This high-level information, however, is not provided to QDMTT-only Jurisdictions."*

An MNE Group **may opt** to have the whole GIR exchanged with all jurisdictions regardless.

## 3. GIR structure — three data-point sections

**Annex A1 (Data points)** and **Annex A2 (Explanatory guidance)** mirror each other:

### Section 1 — MNE Group Information
- **1.1 Identification of the Filing Constituent Entity:** UPE-is-filer flag · name · TIN · role · jurisdiction · recipient jurisdictions for exchange
- **1.2 MNE Group General Information:** 1.2.1 group name, FY start/end, amended-return flag · 1.2.2 CFS type, accounting standard, presentation currency (ISO)
- **1.3 Corporate Structure:** 1.3.1 UPE (jurisdiction, applicable rules, name, TIN, TIN in filing jurisdiction, **status for GloBE purposes**, Excluded Entity type if applicable, Art 10.3.5 jurisdiction) · 1.3.2 Group Entities and JV Group members — per entity: changes flag, jurisdiction, applicable rules, name, TIN, status, **ownership structure** (per owner: type, TIN, percentage), POPE/IPE QIIR status, Art 2.1.3 and 2.1.5 exceptions, initial-phase flag, aggregate QIIR ownership interests
- **1.4 High-level summary** ← **not disseminated to the UAE**

### Section 2 — Jurisdictional Safe Harbours and Exclusions (jurisdictional schedules)
Includes **2.3 MNE Group in the initial phase of international activity** (title corrected by corrigendum).

### Section 3 — GloBE Computations

**Annexes:** B (Notification that the GIR will be received under Exchange of Information) · **C (Transitional Penalty Relief)**

## 4. Transitional Penalty Relief (GIR Annex C) — enriches Module 4's existing callout

Module 4 currently states CD 142 Art 14.3's relief in bare terms ("reasonable measures"). **Annex C fleshes out what that actually means**, and is adopted material via MD 96 — directly usable.

**Key content:**
- Applies to **Implementing Jurisdictions AND QDMTT-only Jurisdictions** — so the UAE is expressly in scope
- **"Reasonable measures" is deliberately not defined**; to be understood in light of each jurisdiction's own rules and practice. An MNE demonstrates it by showing it has, **in good faith, put in place appropriate systems** to understand and comply
- Assessed by the tax administration on the **facts and circumstances** of each case
- **Concrete examples where penalties may be waived:**
  - a mistake of fact that is reasonable in the circumstances
  - errors reasonably attributable to **unfamiliarity with the rules in initial implementation years** (e.g. isolated mathematical or transposition errors)
  - the rule's requirements are **unclear** and the MNE acted on a reasonable interpretation
  - the MNE's actions **do not reduce Top-up Tax liability** in the current or a future year
  - full disclosure of the impugned computation to the tax administration
- **Two important limits:**
  1. Relief does **not** apply to **avoidance, fraud, or abuse**
  2. Relief does **not** remove the obligation to **correct errors and pay unpaid/underpaid Top-up Tax (including interest)** for previous Fiscal Years

**Recommended use:** expand Module 4's Penalty Relief callout with these examples and — importantly — the two limits, since the current callout reads as unqualified good news. The fraud/abuse carve-out and the still-must-pay-the-tax point are material qualifications a user should see.
